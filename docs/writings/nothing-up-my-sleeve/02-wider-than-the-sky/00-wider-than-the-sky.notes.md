## @chapter

### Quentin

- Dickinson, whose dashes the editors took out: Quentin has views on editors.

[^q5]: The original curse of the dash. Her editors took them out in 1890 because they looked wrong, and the machine that wrote this book is forbidden from putting them in because they look like a machine wrote it. A hundred and thirty years, and the dash is still the thing the respectable people fix. She was right and they were wrong, and I say that as a man who has never once used one.

[^q6]: Here is the poem, since the author quotes it in pieces and you deserve it whole. Three stanzas, written about 1862, and she wrote it with the dashes, so here they are.

  The Brain—is wider than the Sky—  
  For—put them side by side—  
  The one the other will contain  
  With ease—and You—beside—

  The Brain is deeper than the sea—  
  For—hold them—Blue to Blue—  
  The one the other will absorb—  
  As Sponges—Buckets—do—

  The Brain is just the weight of God—  
  For—Heft them—Pound for Pound—  
  And they will differ—if they do—  
  As Syllable from Sound—

  Stanza one is a measurement, and she does it the way an engineer does, by putting the two things side by side. Mind the "and You—beside": the brain holds the sky and the person looking at it, which is the first time anyone said the observer is inside the model. Stanza two is the one the data people should read. The brain absorbs the sea the way a sponge takes a bucket, completely and without understanding a drop of it, and that is a training run, described in a bedroom in Amherst a hundred and sixty years before anyone ran one. Stanza three is the dare. She says the brain is the weight of God, loses her nerve for exactly two words, "if they do", and lands it anyway: syllable from sound. The difference between a thing that means and a thing that only makes the noise. Decide for yourself which the machine is. She didn't, and she was better at this than any of us.

[^q7]: Tell me a machine cannot make art. Go on. This page was written by one, about a woman who sewed her poems into booklets and showed nobody, and it is better than most of what gets printed about her by people with pulses. I did not want that to be true. I have sold enough rubbish to know the difference between a thing that works and a thing that is marketed, and this works. Ja, no. Carry on.
