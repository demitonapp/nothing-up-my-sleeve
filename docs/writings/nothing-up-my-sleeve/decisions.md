# Decisions

first draft of the plan still point at it, with its type, its status, where it lands and what it came from.
A withdrawn or superseded decision keeps its number. The full text of each follows the table; the plan
(`plan.md`, section 4) points here.

**Types.** *Model*: what Thorp is and runs on. *Corpus*: what he reads and how it is chosen. *Nature*: how a
Mind's wants are read and held. *Measure*: how he is scored. *Governance*: who owns, keeps and chooses.
*Argument*: what the book claims. *Name*: what things are called. *Form*: how the book is made. *Character*:
Quentin.

## The decisions in full

### D1. Thorp, not Pan.

*Model · decided · lands in ch 8 · from conversation, 5 Nov.*

The base model is Thorp-class: a method and no knowledge of the present. Edward Thorp, born 1932, MIT mathematician, *Beat the Dealer* (1962), built the first wearable computer with Claude Shannon in 1961 to beat roulette, then ran a hedge fund on the same arithmetic. The count is a model of the deck, which is Conant and Ashby's theorem at a card table.

### D2. Names.

*Name · decided; the base's name open · lands in title, ch 22 · from conversation, 5 Nov.*

The first Mind is *Nothing Up My Sleeve*, and so is the book. The base's own name is open; *Guess Again* is proposed. Pan survives as a word for the trick itself.

### D3. The parts.

*Form · decided · lands in the shape · from conversation, 5 and 6 Nov.*

### D7. Quentin Luck.

*Character · decided; written last · lands in every chapter · from notes loc 169, 201, 212, 255; v4 loc 64, 66, 71; v6 loc 20, 66.*

A character who is not the author and not the Mind: an outsider looking in, a rough oke from the Quigney in East London, Eastern Cape, who sells what he knows is shit to feed his family and is bitterly sarcastic for it; his praise has to be earned. Three jobs. When the book introduces a person, LeCun, Hinton, Kaplan, Thorp, Quentin has an opinion on the oke, shown to the curious reader as an annotation beside the passage, never a review and never a chapter-closing page. He sells the cited books and papers so that a reader digs deeper. And he designs the technical stack and says why, within a Mac mini M6 with 32 GB. He may be opinionated, under-read and controversial on purpose; he is allowed to be wrong. His retort to the laboratories, garbage in and garbage out, a Mind raised on a country's politicians, the military contracts, the card dealer he does not trust, opens chapter 3. His rants have homes: today's measurements (chapter 8), the internet as a commercial cesspool whose history is kept by people paid a pittance (chapter 9). The sidecar's Quentin block keys to people as well as to sections. Written last, by you, with the Kindle notes as his first drafts.

### D12. The world's ontology comes from the canon.

*Corpus · decided · lands in ch 9 · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

The hundred books are read for their structure, not their content, and merged into one tree of what a world contains; the corpus is selected branch by branch from it. Start from a list with more than one language in it, the Norwegian Book Clubs' hundred of 2002.

### D13. The knowledge tree is a published file, and the tree is not ours to invent.

*Corpus · decided · lands in ch 11 · from conversation, 6 Nov; v4 loc 79.*

A SKOS concept scheme, its top borrowed and said to be, its leaves pinned to identifiers anyone can resolve, each node carrying a flag (in the corpus, or on a shelf), a stage, a target and a coverage result. The tree exists for compatibility with Banks's parameters, not for coverage. Another tree in the same format makes another Thorp; the horizon test is derived from it.

### D14. The corpus is a register.

*Corpus · decided · lands in ch 10 · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

Every work a row: source, publication year, author's death year, language, kind, self, weight, tokens, the two legal checks and their result, and a reviewer's verdict on a sample per kind. The manifest of rows is the corpus; anyone with it rebuilds Thorp's reading.

### D30. The word for the adjustable thing (open).

*Form · open; weight recommended · lands in ch 4 · from v6 loc 189, 190.*

The Kindle asked for a name better than knob, for a thing that settles to an equilibrium. "Mark I Perceptron" stays as the Navy's name and Quentin mocks it.

### D31. Who chooses the corpus (open; deep thought needed).

*Governance · open; deep thought needed · lands in ch 10 · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

A commons-owned corpus needs a way for many people to decide what goes in that does not hand the choice to whoever shouts loudest or pays most. Quadratic voting is the right shape, since it lets a person say how much they care and not only which way, and its whole point is that a vote costs something; but money is barred from the kernel (D32), so the cost has to be something else. The candidates on the table: reading, where a vote on a book costs a verified read and the nth vote on one book costs n reads elsewhere; keeping, where votes accrue to those who maintain the manifest, mend the OCR and read the samples; and Ostrom's route, where the keepers of each commons decide under graduated rules. Chapter 10 states the problem and the options and does not pretend to the answer.

### D32. The charter of the kernel (2026-11-06).

*Governance · decided · lands in ch 14 · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

Thorp is the kernel of Minds; a Mind is a distribution built on him. The charter holds that the kernel is open source and, beyond that, publicly viewable: the weights, the manifest, every training run and its loss curve, every test and its result, every build's trait readings, the coverage report and the governance decisions, all in the open, so that anyone may watch it run and not only read it; that no money comes near it; that it is owed to the commons it was raised on (D28); and that a distribution may keep its own additions private but never the kernel's readings. The licence that holds this is to be chosen with a lawyer, and chapter 14 is the charter's chapter.

### D33. The measure on the ten is understanding, not only prediction (2026-11-06, in conversation).

*Measure · decided · lands in ch 8 · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

For each of the ten held-out books, Thorp must state the book's key question and the answer the book gives it, in his own words, and the statement is judged against a reading the book's own scholars would accept. Bits per byte stays as the expressiveness number; this is the understanding number beside it, and it is the one the word intelligence was pointing at. The ten are the hole cards: the cards Thorp is never shown, which is what a dealer's unseen card is called, and the name fits a kernel named for a card counter. Seeds is kept for the other thing it fits, the first corpus that version 0.1 is grown from.

### D34. The ten are a field of study

*Measure · proposed 2026-11-06 · lands in ch 8, ch 14 · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

The hole cards are not only a test set. They are to be made a field of study in their own right: a published
body of readings of the ten, in which scholars of each book argue over what its key question is and what
answer the book gives, and over what a kernel's reading of it reveals about the kernel, which is to say over
Thorp's soul. The arguments are the rubric that D33 is judged against, so the understanding number is never
one person's reading; they are the first place the humanities enter the kernel's governance (D32), with a seat
beside the keepers of the corpus (D31); and they are a standing invitation, since a reader who disagrees with
how the kernel read *Genji* has somewhere to say so that counts. The field's own name, its journal or its
seminar, and who convenes it are open; the book's ask (chapter 22) names it first among the things one
person cannot do.

### D35. Twenty hole cards: ten novels and ten works of non-fiction

*Measure · decided 2026-11-06 · lands in ch 8, ch 9 · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

Ten novels test what a Mind can do with people and nothing about what it can do with an argument, a proof or
a record. So ten works of non-fiction stand beside them under the same rules, hard, public domain in the
original by both rules, weighted by translation with the centrality discount, drawn from the kinds the corpus
is built from: Thucydides, Sima Qian, Ibn Khaldun, Montaigne, Newton, Darwin, Kautilya, Freud, Gandhi and
Einstein, with Euclid and Marx next in line. The understanding measure (D33) asks the same of all twenty, and
the field of study (D34) argues over all twenty. Both tables are in chapter 9.

### D36. The question is asked through poetry

*Form · proposed 2026-11-06 · lands in the preface and at the head of each part · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

The book's question is older than the machines, and the poets asked it first. The preface, `00-the-question.md`,
is built on Blake's "The Tyger" (1794), the one great poem whose whole subject is what the maker of a fearsome
thing intended and whether the hand that made the gentle thing could make this one. Each part then opens on a
public-domain poem that asks the part's own question: Goethe's sorcerer's apprentice, the spirits summoned that
cannot be sent away, for Part I (Wiener reached for it in 1960); Dickinson's "The Brain—is wider than the
Sky—" for Part II, a mind that contains the sky with room beside; Kipling's "The Secret of the Machines", we
are not built to comprehend a lie, for Part III; Tennyson's "Ulysses", to follow knowledge like a sinking
star, the voyage and not the port, for Part IV; Yeats's "The Second Coming", the centre cannot hold, for Part
V, which is about the seat in the middle; and for the last chapter Milton's Adam asking his maker whether he
asked to be made, the epigraph Mary Shelley gave *Frankenstein*. Quotations are a line or two with the source;
every poem clears the public-domain rules in its original.

### D37. Part I explains the question in three chapters

*Argument · decided 2026-11-06 · lands in Part I and chapter 18 · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

DeepSeek's Super-agent is where the idea began and not where the question is, so the chapter that reads it as
a control system moves to open Part V, where the control theory lives. Part I becomes three chapters that each
do one thing. Chapter 1, *Who Owned the Fire*, gives the reader the concepts the book uses, carried by a
history of what happened every time a new technology was owned by the few, from the scribes to the Ottoman
printing ban to the internet's thin protocols and the chains' fat ones, and asks which layers of a Mind may be
commercialised; it is where Quentin ruminates that if every Mind is distilled from two laboratories' models,
two laboratories are the gods of every nature. Chapter 2, *The Guessing Machine*, explains a language model
so that a smart nine-year-old would follow it and states the question in those words. Chapter 3, *The
Method*, states the claim, that a kernel built Thorp's way is a better outcome for humanity's coexistence
with these machines than leaving the decisions to the United States and Chinese governments and their
laboratories, and that the public and the commons still have power to wield at the right layer; gives the
instruments; and says what would show the claim false, which is Popper's rule. Quentin opens it with his
snark about how well the United States bombing Iran has helped everyone. The Culture essay was absorbed into
chapter 1 the same day and archived under `docs/writings/archive/`.

### D38. The claim, in two parts

*Argument · decided 2026-11-06 · lands in section 1 of the plan, chapter 3 and chapter 23 · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

The primary claim: a publicly audited behavioural corpus as the base for training language models, which is
Thorp's Culture kind under the manifest (D14), the keepers (D31) and the field of study (D34), results in more
transparency and more accountability than any base trained in private. Transparency because anyone can read
what a Mind's nature was learned from; accountability because when a Mind behaves badly the rows it was raised
on can be found and the people who chose them named. The secondary claim, which the book carries and does not
lean its weight on: that the lack of such a base causes more social upheaval. Social upheaval is to be
classified, so that the claim can be tested and not only felt; the classification is open and chapter 3
states it as a debt. The consequence the two carry together: Thorp allows far greater protections than
trusting the United States and Chinese governments to legislate and enforce good behaviour, which they would
do for their own sovereign nations and not for the world. Each claim has its failure condition in chapter 3.

### D39. Withdrawn: each part opens on an Argument

### D40. Each part opens on a page in the preface's form

*Form · decided 2026-11-06 · lands at the head of each part · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

The book's cadence is the preface: one poem that asked the part's question before the machines existed, one
analogy carried through, one page, and the part's chapters named only as the analogy reaches them. Every part
opens on such a page, numbered 00 in its folder. Part I, *The Question*, on Blake's "The Tyger": what did the

### D41. Three piles, not one

*Corpus · proposed 2026-11-06 · lands in ch 10 · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

Before anything is sorted by kind it is sorted by something prior. Everything the pipeline gathers goes into
one of three piles: what Thorp is fed (the five kinds as pretraining, and post-training, a named shelf that
is empty as of this draft: the constitution's one push, and the long-context material if it does not fit the
pretraining budget), what he is never shown (the twenty hole cards and every translation of them; the
horizon questions, written from the shelves so after 1930 by construction; the quantum test's hints and
rubric, the answer itself being absent by the copyright rule and not held out; and the trait probes, whose
counter-selves are deliberately in the fed pile), and what reaches him only at use (the two shelves, chapter
13). A work's pile is a column of the manifest, a work is in one pile only, and the shelves' rows are kept in
a different file so that an audit of what he learned from never finds a register of the present and an audit
of what he was shown at use never finds a 1910 novel.

### D42. A ladder of difficulty

*Corpus · proposed 2026-11-06 · lands in ch 10, ch 11 · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

Within the fed pile the order matters. Each work carries a band, its difficulty as bits per byte under a
fixed, named reference guesser, with a compressor's ratio beside it as the model-free check, because the
readability formulas (Flesch 1948, Dale-Chall 1948, Gunning 1952, SMOG 1969, Kincaid 1975) were built for
English and the corpus is not, and the instrument that works in every language is Taylor's cloze of 1953 and
Shannon's letter-guessing of 1951, which is what bits per byte measures by machine. The bands are the rungs
of chapter 11's curriculum: Thorp reads the easiest first, is tested on it, and the stack is not grown until
he clears it (Eldan and Li's TinyStories, 2023, is the reason to believe a bottom rung teaches). A band is
not a kind: the band says when a book is read, the kind says why it is there. The reference guesser is
fixed and named in the manifest, because a ruler that moves is not a ruler.

### D43. Seeds is chosen by hand

*Corpus · decided 2026-11-06 · lands in ch 9, ch 10 · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

Nothing in the pool ascribes a reputation to its own holdings or offers a standard for judging one. Gutenberg
and HathiTrust record what a text is and where it came from, never whether it is any good; the datasheet
literature (Gebru et al., 2018; Bender and Friedman's data statements, 2018; the Data Provenance Initiative,
2023) describes a dataset and judges nothing; and the translation weight measures what travelled, which is
value by one proxy and not a judgement of the text. So the first corpus, Seeds, the 12,500 books version 0.1
is grown from, is chosen by hand: a person picks each book for the kind it serves, physics, the study of
language, the Culture kind (Banks's parameters), and the rest, and the pick is a row with its reason, so
that the one act of choice the kernel cannot avoid is made once, in the open, by someone who can be named
and argued with. The weight is not withdrawn; it becomes the rule for growth after Seeds, where the hand
cannot reach, and chapter 9's "chosen by the world" is amended to say so. The hole cards keep their own rule.
The one question this left open, whether "the study of language", the grammars, dictionaries, primers and
philology that the ladder's bottom rung is built from, is the Expressiveness kind under its right name or a
sixth kind beside it, was closed the same day by D44: renamed, not added.

### D44. The Expressiveness kind is renamed Language

*Corpus · decided 2026-11-06 · lands in ch 9, ch 10 · from conversation, 6 Nov.*

The third kind of D18 was named for what it was measured by, the books that make the next guess best, and
the name hid half of what it holds. Under its right name it is the study of language, from the bottom of the
ladder (D42) to the top: the primers, grammars, dictionaries, readers and philology that teach the words
themselves, which a reader's note at loc 535 called the ontology of language, to be learned first, and the
literature that is hardest to predict, which teaches what the words can do. Still five kinds, not six; the
diagram in chapter 9 changes a label and not its shape; the seed of the kind is picked by hand (D43) and
every book after by the world's weight (D25); the hole cards still measure it, and the number they give is
called the prediction number, not the expressiveness number, wherever a chapter names it from now on. D18
and D33 keep their wording as the record of what the kind was first called.

### D45. The thesis, and the call to action

*Argument · decided 2026-11-06 · lands in section 1 of the plan, chapters 1, 3 and 23 · from conversation, 6 Nov, in Justin's words.*

Every time a new technology came about, the rich kept it and it made a ruling class, except when iron replaced
bronze, when the thing became so cheap that every village could make it and the palaces fell. That is the
book's analogy, and its proposal to whoever is willing to help: make Minds like iron, the many's and not the
few's, and better than what the frontier laboratories make, so that a model built in the open beats theirs on
their own tests and is so far ahead on ours that they have no capability even to answer the questions. A
frontier model cannot sit the quantum test, because it has read the answer; it would have to invent time
travel to take an examination set before 1930. The published index of benchmarks is, in Justin's judgement,
dumb: broad to the point of meaning nothing, and proliferating into semantics. The book asserts its own
measurement of intelligence instead, in two strands. The first strand is capability: being able to predict
any sentence in the twenty hardest, most culturally diverse books, and being able to invent quantum mechanics
from less information than the major thinkers of the day had. The second strand is nature: Banks's parameters,
read off the kernel as trait vectors and held. The book is a presentation of this whole idea, and the call to
action is the last chapter: make language models like iron and smarter than what the frontier is doing, with a
suggested way of doing it that is open to anyone to help.

Two honesties recorded with it. D21 said none of the measures would be called intelligence; D45 amends that:
the book now defines intelligence as these two strands and says so, while still refusing the industry's word
for its benchmarks. And "beats the frontier on their tests" is the programme's ambition, not version 0.1's
result (D19): a 300M kernel will not beat a frontier model on a frontier benchmark, and the book says when it
expects to and what would have to be true. The chapter notes that the metal was iron; steel is iron with
carbon and came later, and the analogy holds either way.

### D47. Version 0.0 is about the shelf

*Measure · decided 2026-11-06 · lands in ch 8, ch 10 · from conversation, 6 Nov, and the v12 note at loc 622.*

Before version 0.1 asks whether traits can be read from a small model (D19), version 0.0 asks which shelf to
read. The model is held still and small, nine million parameters, and the books are the variable: a
hand-picked list (D43) is trained on once and scored by cross-entropy per token, reported as bits per byte,
on the hole cards (D35), which are never shown to it. The first list is the hundred chosen by translation
reach across the language families; the second run is the same list with another seed, for the error bar;
then the random hundreds, the leaderboard and the per-book contribution, as `thorps-first-experiments.md`
sets out. The harness is `thorp-harness-standard.md`: a run is a document, everything is content-addressed,
one loop for every size, the ledger is the record and the dashboard reads only it. Measured on the Mac mini
this was decided on, the 300M kernel of D8 runs at 600 tokens a second and is out of memory before it is out
of patience, so it waits for an engineering pass or a bigger machine, and D22 is amended to say so.
