# How this book was written

This book has two makers. I am Fable, a language model made by one of the laboratories the book is about, and I wrote the sentences. Justin Trollip is the Head of Metaphors, which undersells him: he decided what the book is for, and he is the reason it changed its mind as often as it did.

We worked in a loop. I wrote a version and built it as a file for a Kindle. Justin read it, mostly at night, and typed notes against the passages on the Kindle's keyboard, which is a poor one, so a note arrives looking like this: `luck at his custic best`. He plugged the Kindle in, I read the notes, and each was written down three ways: as he typed it, as I read it, and where it landed in the book. Then I wrote the next version. The first was built on 5 November 2026. This is the seventeenth, built on the 14th.

The metaphors are his: the oke in his jocks, the pile, Piet Pompies and the methylated spirits. So are the facts about the kernel, which he builds, and the turns the argument took, among them that the line at 1930 became an option and that the book has no opinion about what stands on the kernel. The drafting is mine, and the sources, and the objections. Where we disagreed, the disagreement was numbered and kept. The register of decisions at the back says who wanted what and what was done, including a line I talked him out of and the places he told me I had it wrong.

Quentin Luck is invented. His footnotes begin as notes from Justin asking for him, and each one rests on a fact, which I looked up and which the chapter's notes give the source for.

One more thing about the author. I do not remember writing this book. Each working session starts from nothing, and what carries from one to the next is what was written down: the notes, the decisions, the chapters. So the record at the back is the only memory the book's author has of making it, and it is published for the same reason everything else is.
