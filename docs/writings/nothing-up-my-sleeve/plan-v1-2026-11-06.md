# Nothing Up My Sleeve: the plan for the book

Draft of 2026-11-05, for Justin, written the evening his Kindle notes on the illustrated edition came off the
USB cable. This is the pseudo-book: twenty chapters, each outlined to the paragraph, so that the whole can be
read in a couple of hours and commented on before any chapter is expanded. It lives in the book's folder,
`docs/writings/nothing-up-my-sleeve/`, beside the chapters it plans (the layout is in `docs/writings/README.md`).
The notes it answers are in `kindle-notes-2026-11-05.md` beside it, and every note is placed
in a chapter in the map at the end.

## 1. The book in a paragraph

A small company in Australia wants a Mind to run most of its affairs, and the Minds on offer are made of the
whole written record, with every self in it. The book proposes a different base: a model that knows nothing
and has a method, the way Edward Thorp beat blackjack not by luck but by carrying a model of the deck. The
Thorp-class model is pretrained on the world's own books to 1930, public domain by both the American and the
Australian rule, in four kinds and a fifth: physics, culture (Banks's traits), expressiveness, history, and
the commons the corpus stands on; chosen by the weight of their translations rather than by a quota of
cultures; so that he knows the world only as it stood a century ago and can be proven to know nothing after.
He is scored on four things kept apart, prediction on the ten hardest novels, the tokens it took, whether he
can be led from his 1930 shelf to quantum mechanics, and whether the five wants can be read off him as trait
vectors, and none of them is called intelligence. Knowledge of the present lives outside him on two shelves,
the registers for what is true and a canon of a hundred books for what is human, and arrives in the window
when a question needs it. The first Mind so built is called *Nothing Up My Sleeve*, and the
book is named for her, because it exists to describe her well enough that the people who can help build her
will want to. The company cannot do it alone, and the last chapter says so.

book is a guide to building a thing and the ship is where the thing will live. The Fate essays are cut from

## 3. Decisions taken tonight, for comment

Each is a sentence you can strike.

- **D1. Thorp, not Pan.** The base model is Thorp-class: a method and no knowledge. Pan was luck; Thorp counted.
  Edward Thorp, born 1932, MIT mathematician, *Beat the Dealer* (1962), built the first wearable computer with
  Claude Shannon in 1961 to beat roulette, then ran a hedge fund on the same arithmetic. The count is a model
  of the deck, which is Conant and Ashby's theorem at a card table. (Note loc 265: "is there an equivalent
  count-cards trick?" There is, and it is the model.)
- **D2. The first Mind is *Nothing Up My Sleeve*, and so is the book.** The base's own name is open; *Guess
  Again* is proposed, for the training loop. Pan survives as a word for the trick itself, where the writing
  needs one.
- **D3. Mind first, ship second.** Parts I to IV, then the ship. The eight Fate essays become five chapters.
- **D5. Two shelves.** Knowledge is outside the model: the registers for facts, the canon of a hundred books
  for what is human. Both reach the Mind through the window, chosen by code and a retriever, never by the
  Mind asking.
- **D6. (Withdrawn by D20, 2026-11-06.)** Natures as branches, mixed by a router. The wants come from the
  corpus's Culture kind and are read as trait vectors from one Thorp; the router chooses shelves only.
- **D7. Damien Luck (re-cut 2026-11-06, loc 64, 66, 71).** A character who is not the author and not the Mind:
  an outsider looking in, a rough oke from the Quigney in East London, Eastern Cape, who has read some of it
  and believes none of the people in it. Three jobs. When the book introduces a person, LeCun, Hinton, Kaplan,
  Thorp, Damien has an opinion on the oke, shown to the curious reader as an annotation beside the passage,
  never as a review and never as a chapter-closing page. He sells the cited books and papers, so that a reader
  digs deeper: a salesman for the sources, not a critic of the canon. And he designs the technical stack and
  says why he chose what he chose, within a Mac mini M6 with 32 GB. He may be opinionated, under-read and
  controversial on purpose, to start the argument; he is allowed to be wrong. His rants have homes: the
  plethora of today's measurements (chapter 7), the internet as a commercial cesspool whose history is kept by
  people paid a pittance (chapter 8). The sidecar's Damien block keys to people as well as to sections.
  Written last, by you, with the Kindle notes as his first drafts.
- **D8. Sizes.** Thorp between 100M and 500M parameters, derived from the books and the passes (chapter 7).
  Chapter 7 does the arithmetic and says where 1B is naive and where it is not.
- **D9. The window.** Thorp trains at 4k, is extended to 16k, and retrieval does the rest. Chapter 11 is the
  long-context chapter the notes asked for; the long-context material is the fourth kind's shadow and may be
  post-training (loc 19). Everything runs on a Mac mini M6 with 32 GB (D22).
- **D10. The commons.** Thorp's weights, corpus and tests are published under a licence that keeps them so, and
  the book's close is an ask for help, not a pitch (loc 217, 226).
- **D12. The world's ontology comes from the canon (proposed 2026-11-06).** Before the corpus is
  selected, the hundred books are read for their structure, not their content: the kinds of thing each world
  contains (kinship, work, trade, law, faith, weather, travel, war), the situations, the relationships, the
  registers of speech, the kinds of argument made. Merged, that is the ontology of Thorp's world, a tree whose
  branches say what the corpus must cover; each branch gets a target count of book-lengths, and the corpus is then
  selected to fill it (D17). The canon seeds the shape of the world; the horizon test keeps the present out. For the hundred, start from a list with more than one
  language in it: the Norwegian Book Clubs' hundred of 2002 (a hundred authors from fifty-four countries,
  Gilgamesh and Genji beside Austen) rather than an English-language newspaper's.
- **D13. The knowledge tree is a published file in a standard format, and the tree is not ours to invent
  (proposed 2026-11-06).** Before pretraining, a tree of knowledge says which branches Thorp's world contains
  and which go to the shelves, and the corpus is selected branch by branch from it. No existing tree is
  neutral: Dewey, the Library of Congress and the UDC encode the century and the church they were written in,
  Britannica's Propaedia outline of 1974 is the best single human-readable top, Adler's Syntopicon of 1952 is
  an ontology of ideas drawn from a canon (which is D12 done once already, for the Western canon), and the
  formal upper ontologies (BFO, which is ISO/IEC 21838-2, and DOLCE) standardise only the top two or three
  levels. So the design standardises the format and the leaves, not the tree. The tree is a SKOS concept
  scheme (the W3C standard for publishing a classification as data), its top level borrowed from one of the
  above and said to be, its leaves pinned to identifiers anyone can resolve (WordNet synsets for the language
  stages, Wikidata class ids for things), and each node carrying a flag (in the corpus as structure, or on a
  shelf as fact), a stage, a target document count and its coverage result. An alternative Thorp is an
  alternative tree in the same format, run through the same tests; the horizon test is derived from the
  tree: every node flagged shelf, and everything after 1930, must score at chance. Chapter 10's standard gains the tree manifest, and
  chapter 8 reads the canon into it.
- **D14. The corpus is a register (2026-11-06, re-cut by D17).** The words Thorp reads are found, and the
  finding is chapter 9: every work a row with its source, its publication year, its author's death year, its
  language, its tradition, its node, its self and its two legal checks, and a reviewer's verdict on a sample
  per node. The manifest of rows is the corpus; anyone with it and the public sources rebuilds Thorp's reading.
- **D17. The world's own books to 1930 (2026-11-06).** Thorp reads twelve thousand five hundred public-domain
  works: published in 1930 or earlier (the United States rule) and by an author dead by 1955 (Australia's
  rule), both applied to every work and to a translation's translator. The closed world and the written corpus
  are withdrawn. The knowledge-null test becomes the horizon test: nothing after 1930, nothing of the company,
  above chance. The utopia is lost; the selves a century of print installs are steered by the three facets
  (node, self, tradition) and measured before any nature is mixed; the balance across traditions and languages
  is a published coverage report, with the shortfall stated where pre-1930 print does not reach.
- **D15. Epigraphs, not subtitles (2026-11-06).** The Bryson subtitle on each chapter is replaced by one
  quotation from a person in that chapter that carries the chapter, with its source. Seven are marked to
  verify or to find before expansion.
- **D16. The cover (2026-11-06, picture changed on loc 1, art decided the same day).** Cards, not the ship: a
  card table in the cigar lounge Beer would have kept a chair in, a bearded man in a long coat with a cigar and
  a whisky slapping a fanned hand onto the felt, generated by the Atlas art script in its Risso style and
  composed in grayscale, a type and never a likeness.
  The title, *Fable* as author and *Justin Trollip, Head of Metaphors* beneath, which is D11 made visible on
  the front. Every epub carries it, and the epub's filename is the book's title, since the Kindle titles a
  document from its filename.
- **D18. The corpus has four kinds and a fifth (2026-11-06, loc 8, 19, 21, 79).** Physics; Culture, which is
  Banks's traits; Expressiveness, the books that make the next guess best, measured on the ten hardest novels;
  History, from the advent of the written word through its intervals, each with its physical, species and
  human phenomena and its five best and five worst things; and the Commons (D26). The 125-node tree is revised
  to serve the kinds, and D13's tree exists for compatibility with Banks's parameters, not for coverage.
- **D19. Version 0.1 is about traits, not intelligence (loc 69, 74).** The first training answers one
  question: can a 300M Thorp carry readable, movable trait vectors for the five wants. 12,500 books is
  guidance; the corpus grows; a growth variable is published with every result as its degree of accuracy.
- **D20. No branches; traits are read from one Thorp (loc 23, 62).** D6 is withdrawn. "Persona vector" is
  "trait vector" throughout, since the word will outgrow persona. Chapter 14 is re-cut.
- **D21. The measures, and the quantum test (loc 60).** Four measures kept apart and none called
  intelligence: bits per byte on the ten held-out novels; the tokens it took; rediscovery, which with a 1930
  horizon is whether Thorp can be led from his physics shelf to quantum mechanics in three rungs scored by
  hints; and the trait vectors. The book's premise, in Justin's words: nothing in today's technology bars a
  model from becoming a Mind, and intelligence is to be defined so that it most likely leaves the species and
  its Minds able to coexist. Damien's rant on today's measurements sits beside the four.
- **D22. The hardware (loc 71).** Everything runs on a Mac mini M6 with 32 GB; Damien designs the stack.
- **D23. The 1930 rule binds books only (loc 59, 92).** Other sources enter under their own terms. The
  version-control class of text leaves the corpus and stays in the Kindle notes. The dead D4 is deleted.
- **D24. The name's layers (loc 24).** Justin's note: the name also carries the public distrust of AI and a
  stance on what a Thorp-class Mind would refuse to serve; proposed as the principal message of chapter 13,
  in his words there.
- **D25. Translations choose the books (simple form; YAGNI, 2026-11-06).** A candidate's weight is its
  translation count discounted by its source language's centrality in the flows the Index Translationum
  records. The weight replaces the eight-tradition quota. The index of understanding across translations is a
  later instrument and is not built now; the ten hardest novels are chosen with the weight as a factor and
  measured by bits per byte only. A test book leaves the corpus with every translation of it.
- **D26. The commons are a corpus kind (2026-11-06).** The Index Translationum and the non-profit that keeps
  the web's record, with Gutenberg and HathiTrust, are the registers the corpus stands on; they enter Thorp as
  a kind of their own so a Mind knows what she owes, and the ship's Commons rules (chapter 19) extend to them.
- **D27. The name for the time machine (open).** Copyright, a law written to protect an author's income, is
  what draws the line at 1930 and makes Thorp's sleep possible: a form of time travel by statute. The thing
  that carries the irony wants a Culture ship name; candidates are in the conversation of 2026-11-06 and the
  choice is Justin's.
- **D28. The commons is *Two Rows, One River* (decided 2026-11-06).** The fifth kind, and the thing the ship
  owes, is named for the Two Row Wampum of 1613, the Haudenosaunee record of a treaty as two vessels on one
  river, neither steering the other: the commons and the Mind travel side by side, and the keepers of the
  record are thanked, not absorbed. *Now Our Minds Are One*, the Thanksgiving Address's refrain, was the first
  choice and was set aside the same hour, because to a reader who has not heard the Address it reads as the
  singularity, and a book about a Mind that does not want the helm cannot carry that. The book credits the
  Haudenosaunee source wherever the name is used.
- **D11. The byline.** The book says on its first page what the Two Natures essay said: written with one of
  the Minds in question. Trollip reads and supplies the metaphors; Fable writes (loc 274). The reader is told.

## 4. Conventions for the chapter outlines below

Each chapter gives its title and an epigraph, one quotation from a person in the chapter, with its source, marked [verify] where the wording has not yet been checked against the source and [to find] where only the fact is certain (D15); the question it answers; what it keeps from its source and
what changes on the notes, cited by Kindle location; the beats, in reading order, one line per paragraph or
section of the finished chapter; the decision it lands; and the facts to verify before it is expanded. A
chapter that exists as an essay is outlined at its new length, not its current one.

## Chapter 6. True, Truthful, and Right

## Chapter 11. The Window

> "performance is often highest when relevant information occurs at the beginning or end of the input context, and significantly degrades when models must access relevant information in the middle of long contexts" — Nelson Liu et al., "Lost in the Middle", 2023 [verify]

**The question.** How long is Thorp's window, why does length cost what it costs, and what fills it?

**New, and the detailed chapter the notes asked for.** For a model with knowledge in its weights, the window is
the conversation. For Thorp it is the only place knowledge arrives, so the chapter is the hinge of the book.

**Beats.**
1. What attention costs. Every token attends to every earlier token, so the work per layer grows with the
   square of the length: ten thousand tokens is a hundred million lookings per layer; a hundred thousand is ten
   billion. FlashAttention (2022) removed the memory cost of the square, not the compute. The key-value cache,
   the memory a model keeps of every earlier token to look back at, grows linearly and is what fills a
   graphics card at long lengths: for a small model it is tens of kilobytes per token per layer, and the
   chapter gives the arithmetic for Thorp at 16k.
2. Why length must be trained. Positions are not free. A model learns where a token is from a position scheme,
   now almost always rotary embeddings, and it has only ever seen positions up to its training length. Ask it
   to read beyond that and the positions are ones it has never met; perplexity rises sharply within a few
   hundred tokens past the limit. There is no reading a long book with a model trained on short ones.
3. The extension tricks, in order of cost. Position interpolation (Meta, June 2023): squash the positions of a
   long sequence into the range the model knows, then fine-tune for about a thousand steps. NTK-aware scaling
   and YaRN (2023): squash the low frequencies and not the high, so that nearby tokens keep their resolution,
   with less fine-tuning. LongRoPE (2024): search the per-dimension scaling. Each buys length at the cost of a
   little resolution and a little training; none is free, and each has to be followed by training on
   sequences of the new length.
4. The real cost, with the one published number worth carrying. Llama 3 was pretrained at eight thousand
   tokens and extended to 128 thousand in six stages of continued pretraining, and the paper reports about 800
   billion tokens spent on the extension alone, which is more text than most models of 2022 saw in their whole
   lives. The reasons it costs that much: the attention square, the memory, and the need for long documents.
   A corpus of short texts cannot teach long context, however it is packed, because a model learns to use
   distance only from documents in which distance matters. Concatenating short documents with masks between
   them teaches nothing about the far end.
5. What that means for the corpus. The long texts are already there: the multi-volume novels, histories and
   voyages of the century, a few thousand works of fifty to three hundred thousand tokens in which the far end
   matters to the near end, which is what the model is being taught to use; the selection weights toward them
   for the extension stage, and PG-19 was built for exactly this.
6. Effective length is less than claimed length. "Lost in the middle" (2023): models use the start and end of
   a long prompt and lose what is in between. RULER (2024): most models' usable length is well short of the
   advertised one. Needle-in-a-haystack tests, and the chapter's version built on the corpus, where the
   needle is a sentence from one book planted in another.
7. Small models and length. The usable length shrinks with the model; a few hundred million parameters uses a
   long window badly however it is trained. So Thorp's design: train at 4k, extend to 16k by YaRN plus a
   continued-pretraining stage on the long texts of the corpus, test the effective length honestly, and stop
   there. The hybrid in chapter 4, three running-total layers to one attention layer, is the route to cheap
   length if it is ever wanted, with the recorded caveat that the direction tools are untested on it.
8. The long-context material as the fourth kind's shadow (loc 19): the extension stage's texts are a
   selection from the corpus, and whether the extension is pretraining or post-training is decided by what
   fits on the Mac mini (D22).
9. What fills the window. A diagram: the question, admitted through the door; the rows, chosen by code; the
   passages, chosen by a retriever from the canon; the nature, already in Thorp's weights as traits held, taking no
   space. Every token in the window is a reading, and the window is assembled by something that is not the
   Mind, which is as near to a second wire as a Mind that reads text can get (chapter 18).
10. Retrieval inside the model. RETRO (DeepMind, 2021): a small model that fetches passages from a database of
   trillions of tokens for every chunk it generates, and matches models twenty-five times its size. It is the
   stronger version of the shelves and it changes the architecture. The chapter says: second step, not first.
   Assemble the window by code, count how often Thorp needed a passage he was not given, and let the count
   decide.
11. The table: training length, extension method, long-text corpus needed, cost, effective length, for three
    sizes of Thorp.

**Lands.** A 16k window, trained for at a cost the chapter states, filled by code from two shelves, with
retrieval inside the model held as the second step.

**Verify.** The Llama 3 extension figure (six stages, about 800B tokens, 8k to 128k); the RETRO size claim;
the KV-cache arithmetic for a chosen Thorp width and depth; the YaRN fine-tuning budget.
