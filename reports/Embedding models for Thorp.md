# Start with Qwen3-0.6B, then test EmbeddingGemma 2

For Thorp in November 2026, the best embedding model to start with is **Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B**. It is Apache 2.0, has a 32K context, supports Matryoshka truncation from 32 to 1,024 dimensions, and runs through the reference MLX embedding library. The ranked top five behind it are **EmbeddingGemma 2** (Apache 2.0, 270M text path, 8K context, released 6 October 2026), **Qwen3-Embedding-4B** (the quality step-up, best run on a rented GPU), **Microsoft harrier-oss-v1-0.6b** (MIT, 32K, a strong multilingual score but thin local tooling) and **Voyage 4 / voyage-context-4** as the one hosted option worth paying for. On public benchmarks the English MTEB v2 top is saturated: six open models sit within about 1.4 points of each other, so licence, context length, Apple Silicon tooling and chunking strategy matter more to Thorp than a leaderboard rank. Two facts decide the shape of the work. No public benchmark scores any of these models on pre-1930 English, so a small in-house test on Gutenberg pairs has to make the final choice. And embedding the whole library on the Mac costs roughly 10 to 25 hours of full GPU time per billion tokens, all of it taken from MLX training, whereas a rented H100 does the same job in under an hour for a few dollars. Every leaderboard figure below comes from model cards or from one secondary snapshot dated 14 October 2026. The live leaderboard was not checked directly.

## Six open models within 1.4 points leave fit to decide the ranking

The open-weight leaders on MTEB English v2 in mid-October 2026 were **QZhou-Embedding (75.97), Qwen3-Embedding-8B (75.23), Jasper-Token-Compression-600M (74.75) and Qwen3-Embedding-4B (74.61)**, according to a secondary leaderboard snapshot taken on 14 October 2026 ([FutureAGI](https://futureagi.com/blog/best-embedding-models-2025/)). The same snapshot puts Microsoft's **harrier-oss-v1-27b at the top of multilingual MMTEB v2 with 74.27**, ahead of the 2025 leaders. The QZhou model card returned an authorisation error, and no primary source was found for Jasper's specs, so neither can be shortlisted on verified facts. Among the verified models the gaps are small: Qwen3-4B trails Qwen3-8B by 0.6 points on English v2. The subscores that matter to Thorp spread more. On English v2 retrieval, **Qwen3-8B scores 69.44, Qwen3-4B 68.46 and Qwen3-0.6B 61.83**, while clustering is bunched between 54 and 59 across almost every model listed ([HF: Qwen3-Embedding-8B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-8B)). For topic spread and distinctness, which are clustering-like uses, the choice of model therefore matters less than for the search box and duplicate detection, which are retrieval-like uses.

Licence filtering removes several strong models. **Jina v5-text is CC BY-NC 4.0** ([HF: jina-embeddings-v5-text-small](https://huggingface.co/jinaai/jina-embeddings-v5-text-small)), **NVIDIA's llama-embed-nemotron-8b is non-commercial and research-only** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/llama-embed-nemotron-8b)), and NV-Embed-v2 is also CC BY-NC. Thorp is personal research on public-domain text, so non-commercial terms are not automatically disqualifying. Under the stated preference for Apache or MIT, though, they drop out of the ranking. The 2026 releases mostly add efficiency, multimodality and better licences, not a jump in English quality. EmbeddingGemma 2 moved from the restrictive Gemma licence to Apache 2.0 and from 2K to 8K context, while its multilingual score stayed essentially flat (61.36 against 61.15) ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/embeddinggemma-2/)). No Qwen embedding successor was found; the Qwen3-Embedding family from June 2025 still ranks in the English v2 top five.

## Each candidate earns its place for a different Thorp job

### Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B is the safe default because its whole chain already works

Qwen3-0.6B is first not because it leads on any benchmark but because nothing in its path is unverified. Its architecture is on the supported list of **Blaizzy/mlx-embeddings**, the reference MLX library ([GitHub](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-embeddings)). Ollama ships official `qwen3-embedding` builds ([Ollama docs](https://docs.ollama.com/capabilities/embeddings.md)), and the card lists 283 quantised derivatives. Its 32K window takes a long chapter whole. Its Matryoshka range runs from 32 to 1,024 dimensions, so Thorp can store 256-dimension vectors for distinctness and keep 1,024 for search ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B)). Two mechanics need care. The model is **instruction-aware**: queries take a one-line task instruction and documents take none, and leaving the instruction out costs about 1 to 5% of retrieval quality. It also uses **last-token pooling with left padding**, so a hand-written MLX batch loop that pads on the right would degrade quality without raising an error. Its weakness is retrieval: 61.83 on English v2, against 68.46 for the 4B sibling. That gap would show in the search box before it showed in clustering.

### EmbeddingGemma 2 is the strongest small model but the youngest and most fragile locally

EmbeddingGemma 2 is the cleanest licence in the field: **Apache 2.0, ungated, with only about 270M parameters needed for text**, made up of a 130M backbone and a 140M embedder ([model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/embeddinggemma/model_card_2?hl=pt-br)). Its Matryoshka retention is excellent. English v2 goes from 68.46 at 768 dimensions to 68.41 at 512 and 67.78 at 256 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-2)), which makes 256-dimension storage effectively free. Google lists MLX, llama.cpp, Ollama and LM Studio support ([Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/embeddinggemma-2?authuser=2)). However, the mlx-embeddings README lists no Gemma architecture, and no official Ollama tag or GGUF repository for version 2 was found, so the local path is **claimed rather than confirmed**. The 8K window covers most chapters but not long ones, and the model is five weeks old. Its precision hazard is serious. The card says not to use float16, because the activations overflow float16's range and can produce NaN or **silently degraded embeddings**, while the mlx-embeddings converter **defaults to `--dtype float16`** ([GitHub](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-embeddings)). A naive MLX conversion would therefore produce bad vectors with no error. EmbeddingGemma 2 ranks second, and it is the obvious challenger in the in-house test. If it matches Qwen3-0.6B on Gutenberg pairs, it halves the compute.

### Qwen3-Embedding-4B buys about seven retrieval points for a rented-GPU run

Qwen3-4B is the quality step-up within the same family, the same tooling and the same mechanics: **74.60 on English v2 and 68.46 on retrieval**, close to the 8B model's 69.44 ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-8B)). A community MLX server claims about 18K tokens per second at 4-bit on an M2 Max using about 2.5 GB, against about 44K tokens per second for the 0.6B model. The repository states no sequence length or method, so treat these as headline claims ([GitHub](https://github.com/jakedahn/qwen3-embeddings-mlx)). At that rate a billion tokens would take about 15 hours of full Mac GPU, so the 4B model belongs on a rented 4090 or H100. On those cards, vLLM runs Qwen3 embedders 2.6 to 2.9 times faster than TEI in bulk mode ([Runpod](https://www.runpod.io/blog/gpu-embedding-workloads-benchmark)). It is the right model for the search box and for retelling detection if the in-house test shows the 0.6B model missing pairs.

### harrier-oss-v1-0.6b has the score and licence but not yet the tooling

Microsoft's harrier-oss-v1-0.6b offers **MIT licensing, 32,768-token context, 1,024 dimensions and MMTEB v2 69.0**, which is well above Qwen3-0.6B's 64.33 on the same multilingual board ([HF](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/harrier-oss-v1-27b); [Qwen card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B)). That multilingual lead does not translate directly to English prose, and no English v2 figure was found for the 0.6B model. Its release date, Matryoshka support and underlying architecture were not on the card as fetched, and the notes found no MLX or GGUF build. It ranks fourth as a candidate for the evaluation, run through sentence-transformers on PyTorch MPS. It should not be a production choice until its English retrieval and its Apple Silicon path are confirmed.

### Voyage 4 is the only hosted option whose features fit books

No hosted model embeds a whole book. Among hosted models, **voyage-context-4** comes closest to what Thorp needs: it embeds chunks of one document together, so each chunk vector carries context from the whole document. It takes up to 120K tokens per request with auto-chunking, at $0.12 per million tokens ([Voyage docs](https://docs.voyageai.com/docs/contextualized-chunk-embeddings); [pricing](https://docs.voyageai.com/docs/pricing)). The 4-series also shares one embedding space with **open-weight voyage-4-nano**. Thorp could in principle bulk-embed locally with nano and query with the hosted models, though that compatibility is a vendor claim that no one has tested on this corpus ([Voyage embeddings docs](https://docs.voyageai.com/docs/embeddings)). Against the alternatives, **Gemini Embedding 2 silently truncates inputs at 8,192 tokens** ([Google docs](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/embeddings)), so a naive per-book call would embed only the Gutenberg front matter. **Cohere Embed 5's 128K window and $0.12/M price come only from secondary sources**, since Cohere's own pricing page lists no per-token figure ([DataNorth](https://datanorth.ai/news/cohere-releases-embed-5-pro-and-embed-5-fast); [Cohere pricing](https://cohere.com/pricing)). OpenAI's text-embedding-3 is unchanged since 2024. Price does not decide the matter: a billion tokens costs **$10 to $200 across vendors**, and about $65 to $100 with batch APIs for the top tier. A hosted model also adds a network dependency to the dashboard search box, and Voyage's quality was not compared with the open models on MTEB v2. Voyage ranks fifth, as a comparator in the evaluation rather than a default.

BGE-M3 (MIT, 568M, 8K, dense plus sparse plus multi-vector outputs) misses the top five on quality, with **MMTEB clustering of 40.88 against Qwen3-0.6B's 52.33** ([Qwen card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-8B)). It is still the best-documented baseline for the evaluation, with a measured **~78 documents per second on an M4 Max via PyTorch MPS** ([Bekko paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25180v1)).

## Chunked book vectors keep the score honest and the training GPU free

A per-book vector must be built from chunks, not from one long input. Even a 32K window covers only a fraction of a novel, and a single 128K vector would blur a book into its topic. The recommended setup follows from this. Strip the Gutenberg header and licence footer, split each book into 512- to 2,048-token chunks on paragraph boundaries, and embed them with **Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B in bf16 or 8-bit via mlx-embeddings**. Left padding, last-token pooling and the query instruction on search queries only are required, not optional. Store **unit-normalised vectors truncated to 256 dimensions** for distinctness and topic spread, and 1,024 dimensions for search, kept as fp16 or int8. Int8 output quantisation is near-lossless with a calibration set, and binary keeps about 92 to 96% of retrieval quality, which only pays off at hundreds of millions of vectors ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/embedding-quantization)). On the weight side, Jina's GGUF tests on a different model found **Q8_0 and Q4_K_M each cost about 0.6% NDCG@5, while 1- and 2-bit quantisation lost 10 to 28%** ([Jina](https://jina.ai/news/optimizing-ggufs-for-decoder-only-embedding-models)). No equivalent MLX measurement exists for Qwen3 or EmbeddingGemma, so 4-bit should be checked rather than assumed.

Each use then takes a different shape. The **distinctness score** is the mean distance from a book's chunk centroid, or better its chunk distribution, to its nearest neighbours in the library. **Topic spread** is cluster coverage over book centroids. **Near-duplicate, second-translation and retelling detection** works at passage level: align the chunk vectors of two books and score the share of chunks with a close match, in the style of the LaBSE-plus-Vecalign alignment used for ancient texts and their translations ([CEUR Vol-3558](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3558/paper6193.pdf)). It runs beside the 5-word shingle hashing, which stays. Shingles catch copied text, and embeddings catch the same story in different words. The **search box** embeds queries live at the 1,024-dimension setting.

The compute choice is set by GPU sharing. A rough estimate for a 600M-class model on a Max-class Mac is **10 to 25 hours of full GPU per billion tokens**, about half that for EmbeddingGemma 2's 270M path. This is arithmetic from inconsistent anchors (an unverified 44K tokens per second MLX claim and a 78 documents per second MPS measurement), and the true figure could be four times either way. On a rented **H100, BGE-M3-class models do a billion 512-token-chunk tokens in about 26 minutes for about $1.53** ([Runpod](https://www.runpod.io/blog/gpu-embedding-workloads-benchmark)). The RTX 4090 figure of roughly two hours is extrapolated from the RTX 6000 Ada, not measured. The practical split is to embed a **sample of 2 to 5 chunks per book locally** (50 to 100 million tokens, one to two hours) in gaps between training runs for distinctness and topic spread, and to send any full-corpus pass to a rented GPU with vLLM.

## A Gutenberg pair test decides what MTEB cannot

Thorp's own evaluation should be small, fast and built from pairs whose answers are known. Build **200 to 500 labelled pairs** from the corpus. Use multiple English translations of the same foreign work, which Gutenberg holds for many classics, as positives for second-translation detection. Add abridgements, retellings and children's versions of the same story as harder positives, and different editions of one text as easy positives that shingle hashing should also catch. Use same-author-different-book and same-genre-different-author chapters as hard negatives. Add 50 to 100 natural-language queries with hand-marked relevant passages for the search box. A 19th-century Danish newspaper study at LREC 2026 found that **the best embedding model depends on the task** ([AAU / LREC 2026](https://vbn.aau.dk/ws/files/833312723/2026.lrec2026-1.287.pdf)), so score each use separately rather than as one average.

Run the five candidates plus BGE-M3, with BM25 and the shingle hash as non-neural baselines; a 19th-century British Library retrieval benchmark also treats BM25 as the baseline to beat ([arXiv 2601.11874](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11874v1)). Report pair-retrieval recall at 1 and 10, ROC AUC for duplicate and translation pairs at book level after chunk alignment, and nDCG@10 for the queries. Repeat the best two models at 256 dimensions and at 4-bit to price those savings. Fold in two engineering checks. The first is a **10-minute throughput run per model on the actual Mac** at a fixed 512-token chunk length, because no published Apple Silicon figure states its sequence length. The second is a **bf16-versus-reference agreement check for EmbeddingGemma 2**: embed a few hundred chunks via sentence-transformers in fp32 and via the MLX build, and require no NaN and cosine agreement above about 0.99. The test is decisive when EmbeddingGemma 2 matches Qwen3-0.6B, which would make it the default at half the compute, or when Qwen3-4B or Voyage clears a visible margin on retellings, which would justify a rented run or an API bill.

## Modern embedders are a curation tool, not a period-pure measure

The first caveat is evidence. **No benchmark scores any current model on pre-1930 English prose.** The nearest results are indirect. A 17th-century Italian study found embedding similarity robust (at least 0.85) across archaic and modern forms ([arXiv 2606.27275](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27275)), and LLM embeddings of Gutenberg excerpts encode literary origin well enough to classify passages by source ([arXiv 2505.17071](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17071)). Pre-1930 English is far closer to modern English than 17th-century Italian is to modern Italian, so archaic spelling is probably a smaller risk than chunking. That is an inference, not a measurement. The second caveat is what the scores mean. These models learned similarity from modern web text, so their idea of "distinct" or "on-topic" carries modern associations. The distinctness score, topic spread and duplicate detection are **curation tools for choosing and checking books**. They should not feed any score Thorp presents as period-pure, such as hindcast or time-travel measures, where the period model's own representations or text statistics belong. The third caveat is style. The STEB benchmark found that semantic embeddings **consistently fail at stylistic tasks** ([arXiv 2606.31741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31741)), so distinctness will measure content and topic, not voice, and authorship clustering needs stylometry or style embeddings. The fourth is the engineering hazards already covered: **MLX has no priority scheduling between processes**, so a local embedding job slows training, and **EmbeddingGemma must never run in float16**.

Several sources are secondary or unverified. All leaderboard ranks come from one blog snapshot of 14 October 2026, not the live MTEB leaderboard. QZhou, Jasper and KaLM specs are unconfirmed. All Mac throughput figures are anecdotal or extrapolated. The 4090 timing is extrapolated. Cohere Embed 5 pricing and context come from press coverage, and Voyage's claim of a shared space with nano is untested. EmbeddingGemma 2's MLX and Ollama support is stated by Google but not confirmed in the reference MLX library or an official Ollama tag. No 2026 LongEmbed results were found for any model.

## Conclusion

Thorp's embedding decision depends less on the model than it looks. The leaders have converged, so the design choices around them decide outcomes: book vectors built from chunk distributions, passage-level alignment beside the shingle hashing, Matryoshka truncation to 256 dimensions, and sampling or renting so the Mac GPU stays on training. Those choices transfer across models, which makes Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B a low-regret start. If EmbeddingGemma 2 or Qwen3-4B wins the pair test, the swap is a re-embed, not a redesign.

The larger implication is about what the numbers may claim. A modern embedder scoring a pre-1930 library is a modern reader's judgement of that library. That is useful for building shelves and catching hidden duplicates of held-out books, but it is the wrong instrument for any claim about what a period-trained model knows. Keeping those two roles apart in the dashboard and the write-ups protects Thorp's central claims at no cost.
