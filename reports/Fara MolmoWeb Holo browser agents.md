# Fara1.5 leads small browser agents, unaudited

**For a Mac that drives your own logged-in Chrome, Microsoft's Fara1.5 (9B, or 4B on a 16 GB machine) is the best-evidenced and best-packaged open model under 32B, but every score behind that verdict was produced by the vendors themselves and no independent party has run Fara, MolmoWeb and Holo head to head.** Fara1.5 (MIT, 4B/9B/27B on Qwen3.5) reports the strongest web numbers (Online-Mind2Web 57.3 / 63.4 / 72.3), is the only family trained to stop and ask before irreversible actions, and has GGUF and MLX builds that run on Apple Silicon. Ai2's MolmoWeb (Apache 2.0, 4B/8B) is the most open to fine-tuning but the weakest to run: F32 weights, no GGUF, no safety layer, no update since April 2026. H Company's Holo has moved from web to desktop benchmarks (Holo3.1 Apache; Holo4-35B-A3B Apache; Holo4-27B CC BY-NC), ships the best documented Mac recipe and the only "your browser" harness mode, but publishes no Online-Mind2Web score for any version and no web score at all for Holo3 or later. Three caveats travel with every number below: Microsoft's own pages disagree on Fara1.5-27B's WebVoyager score (88.6 versus 89.3); MolmoWeb's headline 94.7% is pass@4, not pass@1; and the only human audit in the field cut Fara-7B's WebVoyager score from 73.5 to 62. None of the three attaches to your everyday Chrome profile out of the box, because Chrome itself blocks remote debugging on the default profile.

## Benchmarks come from vendors, use LLM judges and do not line up across families

The four requested benchmarks split cleanly by family, which is the main obstacle to any comparison. Fara and MolmoWeb report web benchmarks (WebVoyager, Online-Mind2Web) and no agentic OSWorld; Holo from version 3 onward reports desktop and mobile benchmarks (OSWorld, AndroidWorld) and no web benchmark. ScreenSpot grounding is the only measure all three share.

| Benchmark | Fara | MolmoWeb | Holo | Who ran it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WebVoyager | Fara-7B 73.5; 1.5-4B 80.8; 1.5-9B 86.6; 1.5-27B 89.3 (MSR blog: 88.6) | 4B 75.2; 8B 78.2 (pass@4: 94.7) | Holo2-4B and 8B 80.2; Holo2-30B-A3B 83.0; none for Holo3 or later; Surfer-H + Holo1-7B 92.2 is a three-module system score | Each vendor, own harness, GPT-4o judge |
| Online-Mind2Web | Fara-7B 34.1; 4B 57.3; 9B 63.4; 27B 72.3 | 4B 31.3; 8B 35.3 (pass@4: 60.5) | No figure for any Holo | Each vendor, WebJudge with o4-mini |
| ScreenSpot-v2 | Fara-7B 89.3; 1.5: 92.3 / 94.0 / 94.4 | 4B 89.5; separate Ground-8B 91.8 (release unconfirmed) | Holo1-7B 89.9; Holo2-8B 93.2 | Vendors; H reproduced rivals internally |
| ScreenSpot-Pro | 1.5: 59.9 / 66.2 / 66.8 with zoom (55.3 / 58.0 / 58.6 without) | not reported | Holo2-8B 58.9; community Holo3.1-4B Q5 59.5 | Vendors; the Holo3.1-4B figure is a hobbyist's reproduction |
| OSWorld (agentic) | not reported | not reported | Holo2-8B 39.9; Holo3-35B-A3B 77.8; Holo4-27B 85.2; Holo4-35B-A3B 80.8 | H Company; one independent self-host got 23% on Holo3 |

The Fara figures come from Microsoft's model cards, repository and paper ([HF Fara1.5-9B](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Fara1.5-9B); [github.com/microsoft/fara](https://github.com/microsoft/fara); [arXiv 2606.20785](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20785v2)). The MolmoWeb figures come from Table 4 and Table 8 of Ai2's paper ([arXiv 2604.08516](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08516v1)). The Holo figures come from the Holo2 card, the Holo4 cards and H's launch posts ([HF Holo2-30B-A3B](https://huggingface.co/Hcompany/Holo2-30B-A3B); [HF Holo4-27B](https://huggingface.co/Hcompany/Holo4-27B); [HF blog: Holo4](https://huggingface.co/blog/Hcompany/holo4)). Fara1.5 grounding in the table is on ScreenSpot-v2 and ScreenSpot-Pro; its OSWorld-G Refined scores (68.0 / 69.7 / 71.3) are a grounding test, not agentic OSWorld.

**Independent evidence is close to nil.** The Online-Mind2Web board mirrored by Steel has no Fara, MolmoWeb or Holo row, and its WebVoyager board carries only H Company's own mixed stacks (Surfer 2 at 97.1%, Surfer-H + Holo1 at 92.2%) ([Steel Online-Mind2Web](https://leaderboard.steel.dev/leaderboards/online-mind2web.md); [Steel WebVoyager](https://leaderboard.steel.dev/leaderboards/webvoyager/)). The only side-by-side tables are vendor-made: Ai2's paper copies Fara-7B's numbers from Microsoft, and Microsoft's Fara1.5 paper copies MolmoWeb's and Holo2's from their releases, marked as not re-run ([github.com/microsoft/fara](https://github.com/microsoft/fara)). The one human audit is Browserbase's 62% for Fara-7B on WebVoyager, against the paper's auto-judged 73.5%, an 11-point optimism gap on the previous generation ([MSR blog: Fara-7B](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/fara-7b-an-efficient-agentic-model-for-computer-use/)). No equivalent audit exists for Fara1.5's 86.6 or 89.3, so a reasonable reading is that auto-judged WebVoyager scores for this family may run about ten points high; that is an inference from one data point, not a measurement. The original Online-Mind2Web paper found WebVoyager's own judge agrees with humans only 73.9% to 78.7% of the time and that the benchmark covers just 15 sites ([arXiv 2504.01382](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.01382v4)).

**The three flagged discrepancies.** First, Microsoft's research article gives Fara1.5-27B 88.6 on WebVoyager while the model card, README and paper v2 give 89.3 (±1.8 over three runs); the gap sits inside the stated interval and probably reflects the article keeping paper-v1 numbers ([MSR article](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/fara1-5-computer-use-agent); [HF Fara1.5-27B](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Fara1.5-27B)). Second, MolmoWeb's 94.7% WebVoyager and 60.5% Online-Mind2Web are pass@4 from five rollouts with the benchmark's own judge choosing, against 78.2 and 35.3 single-rollout; a local user without that judge cannot realise them ([arXiv 2604.08516](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08516v1)). Third, Fara1.5's harness intercepts every `ask_user_question` call with a canned reply and counts a correct stop at a critical point as success, a protocol the cited baselines were not scored under ([arXiv 2606.20785](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20785v2)). Add that Microsoft ran a second pass without Browserbase for tasks blocked by sites, that Ai2 allowed up to 10 retries, and that H's Holo1-7B row in Ai2's table (55.4) is a 30-step figure of unstated origin beside 100-step rows. Treat WebVoyager gaps under about five points as noise. The one cross-family statement that survives is that Fara-7B and MolmoWeb-8B are effectively tied on Online-Mind2Web (34.1 versus 35.3); Fara1.5's roughly 28-point jump over both rests on Microsoft's harness alone. The Online-Mind2Web numbers also depend on the judge: the same agent scores several points differently under human versus WebJudge grading, and one ordering reverses ([Steel Online-Mind2Web](https://leaderboard.steel.dev/leaderboards/online-mind2web.md)).

**Holo's numbers deserve their own suspicion.** H's Holo2 card says external scores were reproduced internally in its own Surfer 2 agent and that bold state-of-the-art marks would be added "once final evaluations are available"; the Holo3, 3.1 and 4 benchmark tables are images, not text ([HF Holo2-30B-A3B](https://huggingface.co/Hcompany/Holo2-30B-A3B); [HF Holo3-35B-A3B](https://huggingface.co/Hcompany/Holo3-35B-A3B)). An OSWorld-Verified mirror lists Holo3-35B-A3B at 80.4% (seen only via search summary), but a user who self-hosted the same model with a simple loop reported 23%, and H staff answered by pointing to a new agent-loop cookbook ([yutori.com mirror](https://yutori.com/leaderboards/osworld-verified.md); [HF discussion #5](https://huggingface.co/Hcompany/Holo3-35B-A3B/discussions/5)). The lesson is that Holo's results are inseparable from H's own loop. For scale, Microsoft's like-for-like run puts a GPT-5.4-based teacher at 83.4 Online-Mind2Web against Fara1.5-27B's 72.3, so the open-closed gap on that benchmark is about 11 points under one harness ([github.com/microsoft/fara](https://github.com/microsoft/fara)).

## Running locally: Fara and Holo have quantised builds with vision projectors, MolmoWeb does not

All three are screenshot-in, coordinate-out models with no DOM input. Fara sees the three latest screenshots and expects about 1440x900; MolmoWeb sees one 1280x720 screenshot per step with coordinates normalised 0 to 100; Holo's conventions vary by generation, with a community implementer reporting a 0 to 1000 scale for Holo3.1 ([HF Fara1.5-9B](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Fara1.5-9B); [HF MolmoWeb-8B](https://huggingface.co/allenai/MolmoWeb-8B); [GitHub xmasdev/local-cua](https://github.com/xmasdev/local-cua)).

One discrepancy in the notes: an earlier read of the Fara1.5 GGUF pages did not find a vision-projector file, but the later file-tree listing shows an mmproj in every bartowski Fara repo, which is the version used here. Add roughly 1.5 to 3 GB for context holding three screenshots; Fara-7B's published workload averaged about 124K input tokens over 16 steps, so image prefill, not generation, dominates latency on a Mac.

Three local-use facts matter. First, nobody has published accuracy for a 4-bit build of any of these models; H's only datum is that FP8 and NVFP4 of the 35B-A3B sit about two points below BF16 on OSWorld ([HF blog: Holo3.1](https://huggingface.co/blog/hcompany/holo31)). Coordinate precision under quantisation is therefore unmeasured. Second, Fara1.5 needs `--reasoning-format none` on llama-server or agent harnesses receive empty content, plus temperature 0 and at least 15,000 tokens of context ([HF Fara1.5-27B discussion #4](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Fara1.5-27B/discussions/4); [github.com/microsoft/fara](https://github.com/microsoft/fara)). Third, MolmoWeb is the awkward one: Ai2 warns vLLM may reduce accuracy, a Mac Studio user hit MPS crashes (the screenshot half was fixed, the MPS device selection is still absent from the backend), and the community MLX build has an empty README and 33 downloads ([GitHub issue #11](https://github.com/allenai/molmoweb/issues/11); [GitHub issue #17](https://github.com/allenai/molmoweb/issues/17); [HF ravilution/MolmoWeb-8B-8bit](https://huggingface.co/ravilution/MolmoWeb-8B-8bit)). No official Ollama library entry exists for any of the three; Ollama works through `hf.co/...` pulls.

## No family drives your everyday Chrome, so plan a dedicated profile

Each family works out of the box only in its vendor's harness, and none documents attaching to a running, logged-in Chrome. Fara's `fara-cli` launches a fresh Playwright browser (Linux and Windows documented; macOS only via MagenticLite's quick start), and MagenticLite runs the browser inside a QEMU sandbox called Quicksand by design ([github.com/microsoft/fara](https://github.com/microsoft/fara); [github.com/microsoft/magentic-ui](https://github.com/microsoft/magentic-ui)). MolmoWeb's `simple` environment calls `new_context` on a launched Chromium; it uses `connect_over_cdp` only for cloud providers ([browser_env.py](https://github.com/allenai/molmoweb/blob/main/utils/envs/browser_env.py)). Holo's current `hai-agents` SDK is the only one with an official mode for the user's own Chrome: it attaches over remote debugging on port 9222, launches a dedicated profile at `~/.hai/chrome-profile` if none is running, and keeps your sign-ins there across sessions ([H hub: Your browser](https://hub.hcompany.ai/agents-api/your-browser.md)).

The reason for the dedicated profile is Chrome, not the vendors: from Chrome 136, `--remote-debugging-port` is ignored for the default data directory and must be paired with a non-standard `--user-data-dir` ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/remote-debugging-port)). So the realistic design is a separate Chrome profile that you sign into once by hand. Routes to your real session do exist elsewhere: browser-use can use the Chrome you have open via the `chrome://inspect/#remote-debugging` opt-in, Playwright MCP has an `--extension` mode, and Nanobrowser drives your browser through `chrome.debugger`, but these expect a general tool-calling model, and Nanobrowser itself recommends Qwen3.8 27B for local use ([browser-use README](https://github.com/browser-use/browser-use); [playwright-mcp README](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright-mcp); [nanobrowser README](https://github.com/nanobrowser/nanobrowser)). Pairing a pixel-coordinate specialist with them means writing an adapter. The shortest path for a specialist is a small code change: keep the vendor's prompt and parser and replace the browser launch with a CDP connection. No Fara or MolmoWeb source documents this; that is inference from general Playwright behaviour and the MolmoWeb code. Even Holo's local mode needs `holo login` and an `HAI_API_KEY`; inference stays on your machine only if you point `HAI_AGENT_RUNTIME_BASE_URL` at your own server, and then "only the agent lookup goes to H" ([H docs: hosted or local models](https://hub.hcompany.ai/holo-desktop-cli/getting-started/hosted-or-local-models)). HoloTab, H's Chrome extension, runs inside your normal browser, but it uses H's hosted Holo3 and no source says it can use a local endpoint ([HN thread on HoloTab](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47776595)).

## Safety lives in the model for Fara and in your harness for everyone else

Fara is the only family with trained stopping. Microsoft defines a critical point as any situation needing the user's personal data or consent: entering personal information, purchases, sending emails, submitting applications, signing in ([MSR blog: Fara-7B](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/fara-7b-an-efficient-agentic-model-for-computer-use/)). Fara1.5 handles these with `ask_user_question` and a form-filling dataset of 8,505 trajectories so that it can proceed after the user grants permission, a case where Fara-7B scored 0% ([arXiv 2606.20785](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20785v2)). All three Fara1.5 sizes refuse 100% of the 111 WebTailBench-Refusals tasks. The stopping is good but leaky: Fara-7B stopped before the critical point on 19 of 23 test tasks and in the other four marked an email as read, liked a post and published a post without confirmation, and Microsoft says Fara1.5's stopping rate is "in line" ([arXiv 2511.19663](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19663)). Prompt-injection resistance is asserted qualitatively only, and Microsoft lists injection among known risks of vision-only perception; its guidance is a sandboxed browser, domain allow-lists and a pause control, and it places unsandboxed deployment out of scope ([HF Fara1.5-9B](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Fara1.5-9B)). The model card also warns against running the model with unrestricted browser access on a machine holding anything sensitive.

MolmoWeb ships no safety layer: the hosted demo's allowlist, query moderation and blocking of password and card fields are not in the released harness, the model was not trained on logins or financial tasks, and its local environment launches Chromium with a stealth flag ([Ai2 blog](https://allenai.org/blog/molmoweb); [browser_env.py](https://github.com/allenai/molmoweb/blob/main/utils/envs/browser_env.py)). Holo's model cards have no safety or limitations section; approval gates, a credential vault, a pause control and a double-Esc kill switch exist in the Agents API and `hai-agents`, and H itself warns that "your browser" mode acts with your logins and recommends a dedicated machine ([H hub: tool approvals](https://hub.hcompany.ai/agents-api/sessions/tool-approvals.md); [H hub: Your browser](https://hub.hcompany.ai/agents-api/your-browser.md)). No primary source on any Holo model describes refusal behaviour or injection robustness.

**Known weaknesses that bear on email and social sites.** Fara1.5-27B's failures are dominated by execution and strategy errors (66.6% to 79.2% of failures depending on benchmark) and hallucinated page state (37.8% to 56.5%), success falls with trajectory length, and run-to-run variance is "non-trivial" ([arXiv 2606.20785](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.20785v2); [HF Fara1.5-9B](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/Fara1.5-9B)). The community test of the 4B on a Mac passed grounding and short tasks and failed both longer tasks (0 of 2, a tiny sample). MolmoWeb's own limitations list names weak small-text reading, action loops and rare use of scroll-in-element, hover and drag; a hobbyist Holo3.1-4B user reports missed tiny icons about one time in three even with a zoom pass and needed loop detection ([arXiv 2604.08516](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08516v1); [GitHub xmasdev/local-cua](https://github.com/xmasdev/local-cua)). Inbox triage is a reading-heavy task, which is the weak spot of every screenshot-only agent. The notes contain no test of any model on X or LinkedIn, so claims about those sites are inference. Every vendor outsources anti-bot handling to infrastructure (Browserbase, stealth browsers, CAPTCHA solving), and Fara's own paper notes low real-estate scores may reflect anti-bot defences; a home IP and a real profile help, but expect to solve CAPTCHAs by hand. Whether a site's terms permit automation, and the risk to your account, are questions the model research does not answer; Ai2's coverage lists terms-of-service handling as unresolved ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai2s-fully-open-web-agent-molmoweb-navigates-the-web-using-only-screenshots/)).

## Conclusion

The practical recommendation for a Mac driving your own Chrome for email triage and routine browsing is **Fara1.5-9B at Q4 to Q6 (about 6 to 7.7 GB of weights) on a 24 GB-plus Mac, or Fara1.5-4B on a 16 GB machine, run in a dedicated Chrome profile that you sign into by hand**, with the harness's Playwright launch swapped for a CDP connection, the screenshot size matched to about 1440x900, the Microsoft system prompt copied verbatim, temperature 0 and `--reasoning-format none`. Add your own gates on top: a domain allow-list, a block on clicking Send, Post, Publish, Delete or Submit without your approval, loop detection and a step cap, and a "draft it, don't send it" habit on X and LinkedIn. Choose Holo3.1-9B with `hai-agents` instead only if you value a documented persistent-profile mode over web evidence; it has no published web score and an account requirement. Use Holo4-27B (non-commercial, fine for personal use) only if you have 32 GB or more and accept an unmeasured speed. Pick MolmoWeb only if your plan is to record your own trajectories and fine-tune on rented GPUs. Do not put your everyday profile, password manager or primary mailbox behind any of them.

The deeper takeaway is that the headline benchmark race tells you little here: the scores are vendor-run, partly saturated and non-overlapping across families, and the one human audit shows about ten points of optimism. What separates these models for your use is packaging, safety behaviour and the harness, and on those Fara1.5 leads while the whole field remains an unaudited research preview. A one-day test of 20 to 30 of your own tasks, scored by a human on the same profile, will outweigh every number above; a 4-bit quantisation accuracy check, an independent Fara1.5 reproduction and a Holo3.1-9B web score are the three missing facts that would most change this ranking.
