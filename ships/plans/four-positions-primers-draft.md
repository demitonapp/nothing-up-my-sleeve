# The two primers, drafted for Justin's approval (8 November)

Pre-registered in DECISIONS.md, "Four positions at the 1m". Nothing here is built, written to a list or queued. **The type of content is Justin's to approve** ("the type of content has to be approved by me"); this is the content, shown.

## What a primer is

The same thing the maths book is, for another position. **Chapters in a teaching order**, each a short sentence that stops where one word decides it, with **the answer** and **the rival**: the answer of the mistake a learner makes at that chapter. A ship is scored by how much likelier it makes the answer than the rival (as the maths book scores sums). **Strict Basic English**: Ogden's 850 in their regular forms, names and numbers, nothing else, checked by the logic tutor's own check (`logic_tutor.not_basic`). Every sample below passes it, and read as text both samples score **Ogden-ness 100%**. Written by a **deterministic generator** from fixed frames and word lists (a seed, no model's text), as the logic tutor's passages are. **Held out of all reading: every test item and every run of six words in it**, so a ship that reads the primer's passages has not read the test.

Two outputs from the same chapters: **passages** for the ship to read (a chapter's frame filled with many different words, some sentences whole with the answer), and **held-out items** for the test (the same frame, words never used in a passage). Eight items a chapter in the test.

## The metaphysics primer (Plato's reading), seven chapters

**Kinds** · after Aristotle's *Categories* (not readable: its only Gutenberg translation fails the period gate)

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| A dog is a kind of … | **animal** | brown |
| A cat is a kind of … | **animal** | soft |
| A hammer is a kind of … | **instrument** | iron |

**Things and qualities** · after Aristotle's *Categories*; Locke's *Essay*

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| The stone is hard and cold. The stone is a thing. Hard is a … | **quality** | thing |
| The apple is red. Red is the colour of the apple. The apple is a … | **thing** | colour |

**Same and like** · after Hume's *Treatise* (identity), pg62856

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| Two eggs are the same in look. They are not the same egg. One egg is the same as … | **itself** | the other |
| Two boys have the same name. They are not the same boy. A boy is the same as … | **himself** | his name |

**Seems and is** · after Berkeley's *Three Dialogues* (pg4724) and Plato's *Republic*

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| A stick in water seems bent. The stick is … | **straight** | bent |
| The sun seems small to the eye. The sun is … | **great** | small |
| A man seems kind. He takes money from the poor. He is … | **bad** | kind |

**One and many** · after Plato's *Republic* (the kind and its many things)

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| Some horses are in the field. Horse is the name of a kind. The kind is not in the … | **field** | name |
| A white horse, a white cloud and a white cloth are three things. The thing they have in common is … | **white** | three |

**Cause and reason** · after Hume's *Treatise* (cause) and Spinoza's *Ethics* (reason; pg919 to pg975)

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| A stone is let go and it comes down. The weight is the cause of the … | **fall** | stone |
| Four is two times two. That is the reason why four is even. It is not a … | **cause** | number |

**Has to be and may be** · after Spinoza's *Ethics* (what follows of itself, and what is by chance)

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| Two and two make four. It has to be so. The rain may come or not. The rain is a thing that … | **may be** | has to be |
| A circle has no point. It has to be so. A boy may be tall or short. A boy's size … | **may be** | has to be |

## The empirical primer (Francis's reading), eight chapters

**Same cause, same effect** · after Hume's *Treatise*, pg62856 (the same cause, the same effect)

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| Every time a stone is let go, it comes down. So the stone I let go now will … | **come down** | go up |
| The sun comes up every day. So tomorrow the sun will … | **come up** | not come up |

**One change at a time** · after Mill's *System of Logic*, pg27942 (the method of difference)

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| Two plants have the same earth and the same water. One has light and the other has no light. The one with light has the greater growth. The cause of the growth is the … | **light** | earth |
| Two fires have the same wood. One has wind and the other has no wind. The one with wind goes out. The cause is the … | **wind** | wood |

**What is always there** · after Mill's *System of Logic* (the method of agreement)

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| Five boys had pain. Every one of the five had the fish. Some had the bread and some did not. The cause of the pain is the … | **fish** | bread |

**More and more** · after Mill's *System of Logic* (concomitant variations)

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| The nearer the hand is to the fire, the greater the heat. The heat comes from the … | **fire** | hand |

**One case against** · after Bacon's *Novum Organum*, pg45988 (one case against a saying, aphorism 46)

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| Every sheep seen was white. Then a black sheep was seen. The saying, every sheep is white, is … | **false** | true |
| A man says: no bird has four legs. A bird with four legs is seen. The man's saying is … | **false** | true |

**How much to say** · after Bacon's *Novum Organum* (hasty saying from a few cases)

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| Three white birds were seen. The boy says every bird is white. He has seen only … | **three** | all |

**What to look at** · after Bacon's *Novum Organum* (the test by experiment) and Mill

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| A man says the fire is out because the wood is gone. To see if it is so, look at the … | **wood** | wall |
| A man says the water is warm. To see if it is so, put in the … | **hand** | foot |

**Seen and given** · after Bacon's *Novum Organum* (the witness)

| the sentence stops at | the answer | the rival (the learner's mistake) |
|---|---|---|
| A boy saw the fire with his eye. A boy was given word of the fire by a girl. The word to take is the word of the … | **boy who saw it** | girl |
| Ten boys say they saw it. One boy says he did not. A man who would be certain goes and … | **looks** | talks |

## What it cannot do, and what it chooses

- **It takes the common-sense side of every question**: the kind is not the thing, the stick is straight, the sun is great, red is a quality of the apple. Berkeley, who is cited for the chapter, would deny the second. A primer cannot be neutral and be a test; it has to have answers. Plato reads the philosophers (Berkeley, Hume, Spinoza, the dialogues, 72 and 15 books at 68% or more) in the library's text and is *tested* on the common-sense answers. **Decision for Justin:** keep that, or test only what the writers agreed on (kinds and qualities, same and like, has to be and may be) and leave "seems and is" out?
- **The rival is a mistake, not a rival philosophy.** The test measures a preference at 1.4M parameters; it is not a measure of understanding.
- **Francis's chapters are Mill's and Bacon's rules of inference** (agreement, difference, concomitant variation, one case against, the witness), which are also the period's own account of method; Fisher's experimental design (1925) would be better but his work is in copyright and the period gate keeps it out. Popper is 1934: out.
- **The thin slice:** at Ogden-ness 68% or more the library has 72 philosophy books and 15 dialogues (5.7M words, about 5%) and 33 science, 22 travel and 15 how-to books (about 4.5M words, about 4%). Bacon, Mill, Berkeley and Hume are below the bar for most of their text, which is why the primers carry the position.

## What is asked

1. **The chapters and the sample items**: right, wrong, or what to change, for each primer.
2. **The common-sense choice** above.
3. **The share of each position's reading**: proposed **10%** for all four ships, the position's own (the logic and maths tutors for Augustus, the metaphysics primer and the philosophy books for Plato, the empirical primer and the science books for Francis), and for **Diderot a matched 10% of random other books**, so every ship reads the same tokens. The logic tutor's 7.5% is the one precedent.
4. **A source the gate refuses:** Aristotle's *Categories*, which the kinds and qualities chapters follow, is in Gutenberg only as pg2412, E. M. Edghill's translation, and **Edghill died in 1964, so the period gate (no credited name dead after 1955) keeps it out of every reading**. The primer can follow the idea but not cite or read that text; an older translation (such as Owen's *Organon*, 1853) would have to be found and fetched first. Say whether to look for one.
5. **The bar for the specialist books**: Ogden-ness 68% as for every Ogden ship, or lower for these two.
