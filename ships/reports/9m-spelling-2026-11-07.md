## (1) Vocabulary: 4,096, 8,192 or 16,384?

**16,384, but only just, and 8,192 is untested.**

| measure (English, lower is better) | 16,384 Endeavour | 4,096 Smaller Pieces | 4k − 16k | noise |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| hole cards, total | 1.6745 | 1.6855 | **+0.011** | ~0.010 |
| Forgotten Tomes, total | 1.6317 | 1.6507 | **+0.019** | 0.0004 (Argo) |
| inside word (spelling) | 1.602 | 1.498 | −0.104 | 0.003 |
| word start | 1.792 | 2.023 | +0.231 | 0.004 |
| punctuation | 2.927 | 3.017 | +0.090 | 0.100 |
| space | 1.620 | 1.809 | +0.189 | 0.002 |
| newline | 0.952 | 1.000 | +0.048 | 0.026 |

**Do not read the split rows as "4k spells better."** `bpb_split` spreads each token's loss evenly over the token's bytes. At 16,384 most words are one token with a leading space, so the cost of choosing the word is smeared over its inside letters. At 4,096 more words break into pieces, and more of that cost lands on the space and the first letter. Inside-word bits fall by 0.104, while word-start and space bits rise by 0.231 and 0.189. That is the same loss moved between columns. Across vocabularies, the only fair numbers are the total and the laws. Inside one tokeniser (the levers and the curriculum) the split is fair.

**Training curve, at equal bytes read.** Total hole-card bits per byte:

| share of training | 16,384 | 4,096 | gap |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10% | 2.131 | 2.264 | +0.134 |
| 25% | 1.908 | 2.009 | +0.101 |
| 50% | 1.760 | 1.809 | +0.049 |
| 75% | 1.700 | 1.715 | +0.015 |
| 100% | 1.675 | 1.686 | +0.011 |

4,096 does not learn faster early. It is slower throughout, and the gap is still closing at the end, so with more reading it might cross over. It also takes 20% more steps over the same bytes (3978 against 3322).

**Laws** (model / reference; closer to the reference is better):

| law | 16,384 | 4,096 | Argo pair gap | 4k spread, last 5 evals |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| non-word rate | 0.086 / 0.015 | 0.085 / 0.015 | 0.019 | 0.085–0.127 |
| Zipf | 0.884 / 0.858 | 0.911 / 0.838 | 0.006 | 0.896–0.925 |
| Heaps | 0.838 / 0.836 | 0.813 / 0.841 | 0.029 | |
| word length | 4.36 / 4.40 | 4.31 / 4.45 | 0.22 | |
| punctuation per 100 words | 21.0 / 21.4 | 20.0 / 20.0 | 1.5 | |
| unbalanced per 1,000 words | 9.2 / 2.9 | 5.8 / 2.1 | 2.1 | 3.3–7.5 |
| capital after full stop | 0.927 / 0.924 | 0.939 / 0.944 | 0.028 | |
| word-length JS | 0.0035 | 0.0021 | 0.000 | |
| punctuation JS | 0.056 | 0.021 | 0.011 | 0.021–0.084 |

On the Argo gap, 4,096 looks better on punctuation mix and balance and worse on Zipf. But within the 4,096 run alone, the last five evals swing more than those differences: punctuation JS goes from 0.021 to 0.084. Every law difference here is noise. The Argo pair understates the laws' noise.

**The 1m pilot agrees** (First Light, English-original fiction, two seeds, Forgotten Tomes): 2,048 scores 1.841, 4,096 scores 1.789 and 16,384 scores 1.705. The seed gaps are 0.015 or less. Part of the 16,384 advantage is size: the bigger table adds parameters. At 9m the 4,096 model has about 3.7M parameters in all, against 6.0M.

**Trait probes** (Smaller Pieces, 22 test books across the two decks):

- Slavic emerges at step 500, above both the bag-of-words and the untrained floors, and holds at AUC 0.84–0.87. That is Russian name spelling, as found before.
- Hellenic is found only at steps 1500 and 3500, and first person only at step 2000.
- Fiction (0.81–0.85) and verse (up to 0.99) never beat their bag-of-words floors (0.85 and 0.98).

Endeavour's probes used a smaller test set and an older artifact without floors, so the two runs cannot be compared on traits.

| answer | confidence |
| --- | --- |
| 16,384 for the 9m rung's own job | low to medium: the margin is 1–2× the noise, from one seed, and 8,192 is missing |
| 4,096 costs about 0.01–0.02 bits per byte at 9m | medium |
| 4,096 does not speed early spelling | medium: it is slower at every point by bytes read |

If the ladder is to grow each rung from the one below on one shared tokeniser (agenda item 2), 4,096 is a cheap price at 9m. The two-fact baseline already uses 4,096 on the loop pool for the pilot, speller and stylist.

## (3) Data: distinct text or total reading?

**Not yet answered by the designed test.** None of the five levers has run. Two pieces of indirect evidence point in different directions.

| comparison | distinct tokens | tokens read | total hole cards | reading |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Argo, the hundred × 4 | 16.8M | 67.0M | 1.800 | |
| Endeavour at step 2044, pool-400 read about 1.2 times | ~54.5M | 67.0M | **1.727** | 0.073 better at the same reading, even though its learning rate was still high |

The Argo comparison also changes the shelf: the hundred is not the pool, and it is far more varied in language. So that 0.073 is distinct text plus shelf. Endeavour's split exists only at its last step, so inside-word at matched reading cannot be compared.

The two readings can both hold. Four passes over 16.8M tokens may simply be too little distinct text for 2.7M non-embedding parameters, while a larger shelf reread up to four times is fine. The levers test exactly this: 800×1, 400×2, 200×4 and 100×8 at 109M read, and 400×1, ×2 and ×4.

| answer | confidence |
| --- | --- |

## (5) What is still open, and the next experiment

**Open:**

1. **Is 8,192 the sweet spot?** It sits between a 4,096 that is still catching up and a 16,384 that leads by about 0.01.
2. **Does 256 cost anything?** This also decides whether a shared context is cheap for growing rungs.
3. **Distinct text or total reading?** These are the five levers.
4. **Does a speller curriculum help?** Mixed, first and mantra, and the mantra is the first test case for named stages.
5. **Measurement, before reading any of the above:**
   - The split cannot compare vocabularies. Use it only inside one tokeniser, or change `bpb_split` to give a token's loss to the bytes in proportion to their cost rather than evenly. Proportional costs would need a byte-level model or a marginal over the pieces, so for now, compare splits only within one tokeniser.
   - The laws at 2,400 words swing between evals by 3–5 times the Argo pair's gap. Raise the sample (for example 64 streams in place of 16), or average the last three evals before comparing runs.
   - Endeavour has the split only at its last step, so its early curve cannot be compared with the sweep's. Re-scoring Endeavour's kept checkpoints (every 500 steps) with `bpb_split` would give the 16,384 curve.

**The next experiment I would run:** `lever-u800-p1` and `lever-u100-p8` back to back, then `spell-vocab8k-v1`. They are all already queued, but behind about forty other lines.

- The two levers bracket the distinct-text question at 109M read, all on one tokeniser, so their split is fair. A result of 800×1 beating 100×8 by well over 0.01 on the total and over 0.003 inside words would mean distinct text buys spelling. That is the trigger for building the per-book distinctness score (agenda item 3), and for scoring shelves on within-book variety and novelty at equal bytes.
- `spell-vocab8k-v1` then settles the tokeniser, which agenda item 2 (growing rungs from smaller weights) needs fixed before any growth run.
- `spell-ctx256-v1` should follow for the same reason.
