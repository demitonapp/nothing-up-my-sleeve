    The claim

    A Thorp model a tenth the size of each 2–4B model chains two facts as well as it does. Chaining two facts is the trick: two premises, neither of which states the conclusion, put together into one that no sentence on the library states. This is how Thorp proves a model can do it, and how the claim is judged.

      How it is measured and judged today

      1

      The test: matched pairs

      Each chaining test is 24 pairs. A pair is a passage that ends mid-sentence, then two endings matched in length and plainness: the idea, which follows only if the two facts in the passage are put together, and a rival, which reads as naturally but does not follow. The passage is split into its premise (the facts) and its frame (the closing words the ending hangs on). There are four chaining tests, one on each of the bettor's chains, 96 pairs in all; a model bets on every pair and never sees any of them in training.

      chainthe passage: premise / framethe ideaits rival
      2

      The measure: lift, what the premise adds

      For each ending the model's surprise is read in bits per byte (so tokenisers of different sizes compare). Its preference is the rival's surprise less the idea's: positive means it bets on the idea. But an ending can win on its words alone (a commoner word, a frame that leans one way), so the same preference is read again after the frame alone, and taken away:

      lift = [ bpb(rival | premise + frame) − bpb(idea | premise + frame) ] − [ bpb(rival | frame) − bpb(idea | frame) ]

      So lift is what the premise adds: zero for a model that only knows which words go together, positive only when the facts before the frame move the bet toward the idea. The share right is the fraction of pairs whose lift is above zero.

      3

      Performed: the mark and the share

      A test is performed when, at the same reading, the mean lift is at least 0.1 and at least two in three pairs are right, so a few large lifts cannot carry a model that is guessing on the rest. The untrained model, same seed, is the floor; untrained and small models sit within about 0.03 of zero, which is the noise: nothing inside it counts as a reading.

      4

      The test is graded before it is trusted

      Each chaining test is the fifth rung of a chain of six (keel to bettor), and a chain is graded only when it gets harder at every rung across a ladder of outside models (Pythia 14m to 410m, Qwen3 0.6B to 4B): no model passes a rung while failing one below it, fewer pass each rung than the one below, and every rung is passed by someone. A test that a small model passes and a large one fails is a broken test, not a discovery. The 2–4B models the claim is judged against never grade the test they are scored on.

      5

      The score: the chaining lift

      A model's chaining lift is its mean lift on the four chaining tests. It is the one number the leaderboard ranks every model on, outside and Thorp alike, read on the same 96 pairs. Below the 50m, where a class is not yet expected to chain, its progress is its climb: on each chain the rungs performed from the bottom, plus the next rung's share of its mark, the mean of the four (0 to 5).

      6

      The forecast: from the classes Thorp can train to the size it can't

      The Mac cannot train a 400M model, so the claim is a forecast. Each Thorp class's best voyage gives one point, chaining lift against parameters, and two curves are fitted in log parameters: a straight line (conservative) and a bending curve (probable, since chaining bends upward with size in the outside models). Each is read at a tenth of each competitor's size. A bootstrap (1,000 draws, resampling the pairs and the seeds) gives a 5–95% band and P, the share of draws at or above the competitor's chaining lift. No forecast is made until three classes are above the noise: a curve through noise extrapolates nothing.

      The method is backtested where the answer is known, fitting the smaller outside models to predict the larger:

      7

      The rule, pre-registered

      The claim is made when the forecast is trusted (three classes above the noise) and P ≥ 0.9 on the straight line for every competitor in the 2–4B range. It is probable when only the bending curve reaches 0.9. Anything less is not claimed. The rule was written down before the readings (DECISIONS.md, "Smoking Qwen, pre-registered"), so it cannot bend to them.
